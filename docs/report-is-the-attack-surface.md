# The report is the attack surface

## 1. The polished report is the dangerous surface

A plausible narrative can hide a claim with no evidence authority, a target with no valuation authorization, or a contradiction created during final editing. Kairosys treats the rendered report as an object to audit, not merely a display layer.

## 2. Failure 1: the model promotes its own source claim

A model can write a confident attribution into a draft. That does not make the attribution evidence. The public slice rebuilds evidence authority from tool observations and visibly discards the model-supplied claim. The analyst sees the attempted claim and the trustworthy path separately.

## 3. Failure 2: complete-looking numbers do not authorize a valuation

Four quarters may be missing, malformed, or contradictory. A target is therefore not a formatting privilege granted by a number on a page. Valuation authorization follows a coherent financial state; when it is absent, the target is removed and the surviving material becomes context only.

## 4. Failure 3: final prose introduces a new contradiction

Even when earlier checks pass, a rendered report can state a target, an EPS value, and a displayed multiple that do not agree. The rendered-report audit checks the final relationship. This is why a report needs a final audit after generation, not just careful inputs before it.

## 5. Why a failed audit can retain a labeled draft

Not every failed audit discards all output. When a finding limits a claim without making the remaining background misleading, a retained draft can preserve useful context for analyst salvage. It stays visibly labeled and review-capped; a blocked target is removed rather than smuggled through. Contradictory or insufficient material is withheld.

That nuance matters: the system is not trying to make every draft look deliverable. It is making the delivery meaning explicit so an analyst knows what can be edited, what needs review, what is context only, and what must not move forward.

## 6. What the clean-room demo proves—and does not

The [offline browser dossier](../web/index.html) proves five fixed synthetic scenarios exercise evidence authority, valuation authorization, and rendered-report audit behavior. It does not establish broad financial coverage, replace analyst judgment, or represent a complete system.
